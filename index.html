The cell is the smallest unit that still counts as alive.

This reference walks through what is inside a cell, how prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells differ, how cells are classified, and how a general cell changes shape and structure to specialize for a single job.

Part 01 — Organelles

Structure and function of major subcellular organelles

Covers both eukaryotic and prokaryotic organelles.

Eukaryotic cells

Structure Description Function
Cell membrane Thin and flexible layer around the cell. Controls what enters and leaves the cell.
Cytoplasm Jelly-like substance inside the cell. Holds organelles and is where many chemical reactions occur.
Nucleus Membrane-bound structure that contains DNA. Controls cell activities and stores genetic information.
Nucleolus The dense region inside the nucleus. Responsible for the production of ribosomal components.
Ribosomes Small structures composed of RNA and proteins. Responsible for protein production.
Mitochondria Bean-shaped organelles with folded inner membranes. Produces energy in the form of adenosine triphosphate (ATP) for the cell.
Endoplasmic reticulum (ER) A network of membranes. Makes and transports proteins and lipids.
Golgi apparatus Stack of flattened membrane sacs. Modifies, sorts and packages proteins.
Lysosomes Small membrane-bound sacs containing enzymes. Breaks down waste and damaged cell parts.
Vacuoles Membrane-bound storage sacs. Stores water, nutrients and waste.
Cytoskeleton Network of protein fibers. Maintains cell shape and helps movement.
Chloroplast An organelle with a distinctly green pigment containing chlorophyll. Performs photosynthesis.
Cell wall Rigid outer layer outside the membrane. Supports and protects the cell.

Prokaryotic cells

Structure Description Function Cell City Analogy
Plasma membrane Thin phospholipid bilayer containing proteins. Controls what enters and leaves the cell; involved in respiration, energy production, and cell signaling. City Security Gate
Cell wall Rigid layer outside the plasma membrane. In bacteria, mainly made of peptidoglycan. Maintains cell shape and protects the cell from bursting due to osmotic pressure. Protective City Barrier
Capsule / slime layer Sticky outer layer made mainly of polysaccharides or proteins. Protects against drying and immune defenses; helps cells attach to surfaces and form biofilms. Protective Shield
Cytoplasm Jelly-like interior containing water, enzymes, ions, nutrients, and cellular components. Site of many metabolic reactions. Main City Workspace
Nucleoid Region containing the cell's main circular DNA chromosome; not surrounded by a membrane. Stores genetic information and controls cellular activities. Central Archive
Plasmids Small, usually circular pieces of DNA separate from the main chromosome. Carry additional genes, such as antibiotic resistance genes; can be transferred between cells. Extra Instruction Pamphlets
Ribosomes Small complexes of rRNA and proteins; prokaryotic ribosomes are 70S. Manufacture proteins by translating mRNA. Protein Factories
Flagellum Long, helical protein filament attached to a basal body and motor. Provides movement or locomotion. City Propeller
Pili / fimbriae Short protein filaments extending from the cell surface. Help cells attach to surfaces; some pili enable DNA transfer between bacteria. Grappling Hooks & Ropes
Inclusion bodies Storage granules or particles within the cytoplasm. Store nutrients and energy reserves such as glycogen, lipids, or phosphate. Storage Depots
Part 02 — Comparison

Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, by distinguishing feature

Twelve features compared side by side.

Feature Prokaryotic cells Eukaryotic cells
Definition Single-cell organisms, no nucleus. Complex organisms, with nucleus.
Examples Bacteria, archaea. Animals, plants, fungi.
Cell size Smaller, typically 0.1–5.0 µm in diameter. Larger, usually 10–100 µm in diameter.
Mitochondria Absent; energy produced in the cytoplasm. Present; powerhouse of the cell.
Cytoplasm Present; site of metabolic processes. Present; holds organelles in place.
Endoplasmic reticulum Absent; simpler protein synthesis. Present; crucial for protein synthesis.
Plasmids Present; small, circular DNA. Rare, except in some fungi and plants.
Nucleus Absent; DNA floats freely. Present; contains and protects.
DNA organelles Few; simpler structures. Many specialized functions.
DNA structure Single, circular chromosome. Multiple, linear chromosomes.
Ribosomes 70S; smaller. 80S; larger.
Locomotion Simple flagella; direct movement. Complex flagella or cilia for movement.

Part 05 — Specialization

Cell modifications that enable specialization

How structure changes to fit function.

Cell modifications are the changes in a cell's structure, shape, size, and cellular components that let it perform a specific function efficiently. As cells specialize, they develop unique features suited to their particular roles, which allows different cell types to work together and maintain the normal processes of an organism.

Cell type Modification How it helps the cell function
Red blood cells (erythrocytes) Lose their nucleus and develop a biconcave shape. Allows them to carry and transport oxygen more efficiently.
Nerve cells (neurons) Develop long extensions called axons and dendrites. Enables the transmission of electrical signals throughout the body.
Muscle cells Contain numerous mitochondria and specialized contractile proteins. Provide energy and allow the cells to contract and produce movement.
Root hair cells Develop long, thin extensions that increase surface area. Enhances the absorption of water and minerals from the soil.
